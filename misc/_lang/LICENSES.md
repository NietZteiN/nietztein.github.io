# Sources and licences of the Japanese data kit

Every data file in this folder is generated by a script in `scripts/lang/` from sources fetched on the days given below. The parts between the "generated" marks are written by `scripts/lang/build-all.mjs` from each file's own `meta`, so they cannot drift from the data: source address, SHA-256 of what was fetched, date, what was kept, what was left out or changed, and the credit a page must show.

The files derived from material under a Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike licence are offered under CC BY-SA 4.0 in turn (the licence text is `LICENSE-CC-BY-SA-4.0` at the root of the site, and <https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode> at Creative Commons). That covers every file here except the Soseki text, which is in the public domain.

A page shows the credit of each file it loads in its "How it works" footer, word for word. Beside it the page links the licence name to the licence text (<https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/>; for the Wikisource page of the poems, which is under version 3.0, <https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/>) and says that the data was changed, with a link to this file: the lists headed "Left out or changed" below are the statement of changes that the licence asks for.

## hyakunin.js

Four wiki pages, each at a pinned revision (the revisions are in `scripts/lang/hyakunin.lock.json`), and KANJIDIC2.

- **Japanese Wikisource**, the page of the Ogura Hyakunin Isshu: the poems as printed, their readings in historical kana, the poets and their readings, the anthology each poem comes from, and the prefix the page prints in bold as the kimariji. The poems, of the seventh to the thirteenth century, are in the public domain. The page itself (the readings and the table) carries the tag CC BY-SA 3.0 Unported; attribute it to the contributors of Japanese Wikisource.
- **Japanese Wikipedia**, the article on the Hyakunin Isshu: its table of the hundred poems, read as a second witness for the readings and the poets' names. CC BY-SA 4.0; attribute it to the contributors of Japanese Wikipedia. Seven strings in five poems are taken from it where the Wikisource page is wrong or prints a reading that is not the one in use for the game; they are listed below and in `meta.corrections`, each with what the Wikisource page prints. The other places where the two pages differ are kept in `meta.variants`, with the table's wording beside the file's. The revision pinned is the text the article has had since 31 August 2026 (the edit of 5 October 2026 that carries the revision number undid a change of the same day to another section).
- **KANJIDIC2** of the Electronic Dictionary Research and Development Group (CC BY-SA 4.0, <https://www.edrdg.org/edrdg/licence.html>): its cross-references say which kanji is another form of a joyo kanji. Ten kanji that the Wikisource page prints in an older form are written in the joyo form on that authority; they are listed below and in `meta.forms`.
- **English Wikisource**, William N. Porter, *A Hundred Verses from Old Japan* (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1909): the transcription of the 200 pages that hold the poems. Public domain: published before 1931; English Wikisource records Porter's death in 1929 and tags the work PD-US.
- **Japanese Wikipedia**, the article on kimariji: its list of the hundred kimariji, spelled by sound. CC BY-SA 4.0; attribute it to the contributors of Japanese Wikipedia. The list is kept as `published.bySound`, and the computed kimariji are tested against it. The names it prints for two poets are one of the checks on the corrections.

The kimariji themselves are computed, not copied; `README.md` gives the rule.

The file as a whole is offered under CC BY-SA 4.0, which the 3.0 licence of the Wikisource page allows for an adaptation.

## kanji.js

KANJIDIC2, KRADFILE and RADKFILE of the Electronic Dictionary Research and Development Group (EDRDG), fetched from the Group's servers. Licence: CC BY-SA 4.0, under the Group's licence statement, <https://www.edrdg.org/edrdg/licence.html>. The statement asks a site that shows material from the files to acknowledge them on the page, and to keep the data up to date (`node scripts/lang/build-all.mjs --refresh`).

Section 8 of the statement lists parts of KANJIDIC2 that belong to other people (the SKIP codes, the four-corner codes, the Morohashi numbers, the Spahn and Hadamitzky descriptors, the De Roo codes, the pinyin and the Korean readings). None of them is taken.

KRADFILE2, which extends KRADFILE to further kanji, is copyright James Rose and is not under the Group's licence. It is not used, which is why two joyo kanji it would cover have no parts here.
