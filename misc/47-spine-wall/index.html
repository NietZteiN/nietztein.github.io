…. The bookcases are the real ones, in the site's order, shelf by shelf, book by book in catalogue position; the wood, the brass shelf plates and the clutter are drawn from the owner's descriptions. Spine sizes are assumed from type (manga 14 mm, bunko 12 mm, paperback 24 mm, hardback 34 mm, art book 32 mm, textbook 40 mm, magazine 8 mm, with a little hashed variation), so the "metres of shelf" are an estimate from those widths, not a tape measure. Colours and finishes come from publisher series where known (Harvard Classics, bunko, tankōbon, Penguin, Oxford, Vintage, ATI…) and otherwise from a stable hash of the catalogue id. Data: assets/data/library.json. No libraries, nothing leaves the browser.

Spine Wall

One long wall: every bookcase in the house side by side, every shelf, every spine in its real position and at roughly its real size. Zoom in until you can read the titles.

  • drag or ← → scroll along the wall (with inertia); ↑ ↓ pan when zoomed in
  • wheel, pinch, + − zoom; shift+wheel scrolls; 0 fits the whole wall; double-click a spine to zoom to it
  • K · H · N … jump to a bookcase; the strip at the bottom is a minimap, click or drag it
  • / search a title or author (accent-insensitive; kana and kanji verbatim). The torch flies to the match; Enter or N steps through matches; Esc turns it off
  • hover a spine for the tooltip, click for the card and its link to the Bookshelf tab
  • X-ray repaints the spines by genre (the site's hues), language, era (decade bands), whether a free e-text exists, or every book by the selected author; L hides the titles for a pure colour view
  • save view exports what you see as a PNG; save wall renders the entire wall in slices to one tall stitched PNG

Renders in 256 px tiles cached per zoom level so scrolling stays smooth; titles are drawn only once a spine is wide enough to read. Everything is deterministic from the catalogue ids, so the wall is the same on every visit.
