## 1. [Can Watermarks Survive Translation? On the Cross-lingual Consistency of Text Watermark for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00013)

**Authors:** Zhiwei He, Binglin Zhou, Hongkun Hao, Aiwei Liu, Xing Wang et al.  
**Categories:** cs.CL, cs.CR (new)  
**Score:** 28. Matched: cross-lingual [title], watermark [title], text watermark [title], translation [title], language model [title], large language model [title]  
**arXiv:** 2609.00013 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00013), [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00013))

Text watermarks for large language models are usually evaluated in one language. We introduce cross-lingual consistency, the ability of a watermark to remain detectable after the text is translated into another language, and find that current watermarking methods lose almost all of their strength under translation. We propose a cross-lingual watermark removal attack and a defense that shares watermark keys across semantically equivalent tokens in multiple languages.

## 2. [Towards Automated Circuit Discovery for Mechanistic Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00017)

**Authors:** Arthur Conmy, Augustine N. Mavor-Parker, Aengus Lynch, Stefan Heimersheim, Adria Garriga-Alonso  
**Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.CL (cross)  
**Score:** 26. Matched: mechanistic interpretability [title], circuit [title], circuit discovery [title], interpretability [title]  
**arXiv:** 2609.00017 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00017), [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00017))

Mechanistic interpretability work follows a workflow of choosing a task, a metric and a granularity, then pruning the model to find the subgraph that implements the behavior. We automate the pruning step with ACDC, an algorithm that recovers previously published circuits in GPT-2 small, including the indirect object identification circuit.

## 5. [Steering Llama 2 via Contrastive Activation Addition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00004)

**Authors:** Nina Rimsky, Nick Gabrieli, Julia Schulz, Meg Tong, Evan Hubinger et al.  
**Categories:** cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG (new)  
**Score:** 19.5. Matched: activation addition [title], steering vector, steering [title], residual stream, language model  
**arXiv:** 2609.00004 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00004), [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00004))

We introduce contrastive activation addition, a method for steering language models by adding a vector to the residual stream at inference time. Steering vectors are computed from the difference in activations between positive and negative examples of a behavior. The approach modulates sycophancy, hallucination and refusal in Llama 2 with little loss of capability.

## 6. [Sparse Autoencoders Find Highly Interpretable Features in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00001)

**Authors:** Hoagy Cunningham, Aidan Ewart, Logan Riggs, Robert Huben, Lee Sharkey  
**Categories:** cs.LG, cs.CL (new)  
**Score:** 19. Matched: sparse autoencoder [title], circuit, superposition, residual stream, language model [title]  
**arXiv:** 2609.00001 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00001), [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00001))

Neural networks store many concepts in superposition, which makes single neurons hard to read. We train sparse autoencoders on residual stream activations and find that the learned dictionary features are far more interpretable than neurons, as judged by automatic and human evaluation. We also show that the features can be used to localize and edit the behavior of a circuit responsible for indirect object identification.

## 8. [Attribution Patching Outperforms Automated Circuit Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00016)

**Authors:** Aaquib Syed, Can Rager, Arthur Conmy  
**Categories:** cs.LG, cs.CL (new)  
**Score:** 18. Matched: circuit [title], circuit discovery [title], attribution patching [title]  
**arXiv:** 2609.00016 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00016), [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00016))

Automated circuit discovery methods such as ACDC are slow because they require a forward pass per edge. We show that a linear approximation, attribution patching, estimates edge importance with a single backward pass and recovers circuits at least as faithful as those from ACDC on standard tasks at a fraction of the cost.

We propose TOFU, a benchmark for machine unlearning built from fictitious author profiles that no model has seen during pretraining. The benchmark measures forget quality and model utility after unlearning. Existing unlearning methods fail to match a model retrained without the forget set.

## 10. [Do Llamas Work in English? On the Latent Language of Multilingual Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00018)

**Authors:** Chris Wendler, Veniamin Veselovsky, Giovanni Monea, Robert West  
**Categories:** cs.CL, cs.LG (new)  
**Score:** 15.5. Matched: multilingual [title], cross-lingual, logit lens, translation, language model  
**arXiv:** 2609.00018 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00018), [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00018))

We ask whether multilingual language models use English as an internal pivot language. Applying the logit lens to Llama 2 on translation and cloze prompts in several languages, we find that intermediate layers assign high probability to the English version of the next token before the final layers commit to the target language. We discuss what this means for cross-lingual bias.
